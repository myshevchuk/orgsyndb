* Summary
Ethyl (E)-cinnamate by a solvent-free Wittig olefination of benzaldehyde
with the stabilised ylide ethyl (triphenylphosphoranylidene)acetate at
room temperature. The ester is separated from triphenylphosphine oxide by
trituration with heptane. The product is the starting material of
OSDB-0008. This is sample data: the quantities follow the textbook
method but are not experimentally verified.

* Procedure
1. Weigh the ethyl (triphenylphosphoranylidene)acetate (2.09 g,
   6.00 mmol) into a 25 mL conical flask containing a small magnetic
   stirring bar.
2. Add the benzaldehyde (0.531 g, 0.509 mL, 5.00 mmol) from a
   graduated pipette, directly onto the solid (Note 1).
3. Stir the paste at room temperature for 20 minutes, scraping the walls
   with a spatula from time to time. The mixture becomes a thick, nearly
   homogeneous slurry as the reaction proceeds (Note 2).
4. Add heptane (15 mL) and stir vigorously for 5 minutes. The ester
   dissolves, and triphenylphosphine oxide remains as a white powder.
5. Filter the suspension through a small plug of cotton wool in a
   Pasteur pipette or through a fluted filter paper, and rinse the solid
   with heptane in two 5 mL portions (Note 3).

* Workup and Purification
Evaporate the combined heptane filtrates on a rotary evaporator (bath at
40 °C). If more white solid separates from the residue, take it up in
heptane (5 mL), filter again and evaporate. The product remains as a
colourless to pale yellow oil. Expected yield 0.705 g (4.00 mmol, 80 %).

* Characterization
Colourless to pale yellow oil with a sweet, balsamic odour. Determine the
E:Z ratio from the ¹H NMR spectrum. Expected signals of the (E)-isomer
(CDCl₃): δ 7.69 (d, J = 16.0 Hz, 1H), 7.55–7.50 (m, 2H), 7.40–7.36 (m,
3H), 6.44 (d, J = 16.0 Hz, 1H), 4.27 (q, J = 7.1 Hz, 2H), 1.34 (t,
J = 7.1 Hz, 3H). The (Z)-isomer shows its vinylic doublets with a
smaller coupling constant, near 12.5 Hz. The IR spectrum (neat film)
shows the conjugated ester C=O stretch near 1710 cm⁻¹ and the C=C
stretch near 1637 cm⁻¹.

* Notes
1. Benzaldehyde oxidises in air to benzoic acid; use a freshly opened or
   recently distilled sample.
2. Stabilised ylides react with aldehydes without a solvent or a strong
   base and give mainly the (E)-alkene.
3. Triphenylphosphine oxide is almost insoluble in cold heptane, which is
   what makes this simple separation work.

* Hazards and Waste Disposal
Benzaldehyde is harmful if swallowed. The ylide irritates skin, eyes and
the respiratory tract; weigh it in a fume hood. Heptane is highly
flammable, may cause drowsiness, and may be fatal if swallowed and it
enters the airways; keep it away from ignition sources. Triphenylphosphine
oxide is harmful if swallowed. Collect the triphenylphosphine oxide as
solid organic waste, and the heptane distillate as non-halogenated
organic waste.
