## Summary

Isoborneol from camphor. This is sample data; the quantities are not experimentally verified.

## Reagents

| Reagent | CAS | MW (g/mol) | Amount | mmol | Equiv |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Camphor (racemic) | 76-22-2 | 152.24 | 1.522 g | 10.0 | 1.0 |
| Sodium borohydride | 16940-66-2 | 37.83 | 0.189 g | 5.0 | 0.5 |
| Methanol | 67-56-1 | 32.04 | 15 mL | – | – |

## Procedure

1. Dissolve camphor in methanol in a flask.
2. Cool the solution in a water bath.
3. Add sodium borohydride in small portions with stirring.
4. Stir for 1 hour at room temperature.
5. Cool in ice.
6. Add water cautiously, allowing gas evolution to subside.

## Workup and Purification

Add further water to complete precipitation. Collect the solid by suction filtration, wash with water and dry. The product is an isoborneol-rich mixture containing borneol. Expected yield of the mixture: 1.08 g (7.00 mmol, 70 %); a typical isoborneol:borneol ratio is about 85:15 by ¹H NMR.

## Characterization

White solid; IR (ATR): broad O–H near 3300 cm⁻¹. Determine the isoborneol-to-borneol ratio by integrating their distinct CH–OH signals in the ¹H NMR spectrum, near δ 3.6 and 4.0 respectively.

## Notes

1. The reduction is not stereoselective enough to give a single alcohol; report the NMR ratio with the yield.
2. Check the identity and purity of the product before further use.

## Hazards and Waste Disposal

Camphor (racemic): flammable; harmful if swallowed; Sodium borohydride: corrosive; releases flammable hydrogen with water or acid; Methanol: highly flammable and toxic; causes organ damage; Isoborneol (racemic): dust or vapour may irritate. Work in a fume hood with gloves and eye protection. Collect aqueous and organic waste separately according to local rules.
