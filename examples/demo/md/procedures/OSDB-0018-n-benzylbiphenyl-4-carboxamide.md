## Summary

Boric acid catalyses formation of N-benzylbiphenyl-4-carboxamide from the acid and benzylamine. This is sample data; the quantities are not experimentally verified.

## Reagents

| Reagent | CAS | MW (g/mol) | Amount | mmol | Equiv |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Biphenyl-4-carboxylic acid | 92-92-2 | 198.22 | 0.991 g | 5 | 1.0 |
| Benzylamine | 100-46-9 | 107.16 | 0.643 g | 6 | 1.2 |
| Boric acid | 10043-35-3 | 61.83 | 0.062 g | cat. | cat. |
| Toluene | 108-88-3 | 92.14 | 35 mL | – | – |
| Ethanol | 64-17-5 | 46.07 | 10 mL | – | – |

## Procedure

1. Charge a flask with biphenyl-4-carboxylic acid, benzylamine and boric acid.
2. Add toluene and a stirring bar.
3. Fit a Dean–Stark trap and reflux condenser, open to the atmosphere.
4. Heat at reflux with stirring for about 16 hours.
5. Allow the mixture to cool, then concentrate it.
6. Add ethanol and warm to dissolve the crude product.

## Workup and Purification

Cool the ethanol solution to room temperature and then in ice. Collect the crystals by suction filtration and dry. Expected yield: 1.01 g (3.50 mmol, 70 %).

## Characterization

White solid; expected IR amide bands near 3300 (N–H) and 1650 cm⁻¹ (C=O).

## Notes

This procedure adapts Tang’s method to biphenyl-4-carboxylic acid.

1. The trap removes water formed during amidation.
2. Check the heating apparatus before the extended reflux.
3. Use only enough ethanol to dissolve the crude solid when hot.

## Hazards and Waste Disposal

Biphenyl-4-carboxylic acid: may irritate skin and eyes; Benzylamine: corrosive and harmful by inhalation; Boric acid: may damage fertility or the unborn child; Toluene: highly flammable; harmful by inhalation and suspected of damaging the unborn child; Ethanol: highly flammable; eye irritant; N-Benzylbiphenyl-4-carboxamide: may irritate skin and eyes. Work in a fume hood; wear gloves and eye protection. Collect toluene and ethanol mother liquors as organic waste.
