## Summary

Camphor from borneol. This is sample data; the quantities are not experimentally verified.

## Reagents

| Reagent | CAS | MW (g/mol) | Amount | mmol | Equiv |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Borneol (racemic) | 507-70-0 | 154.25 | 1.543 g | 10.0 | 1.0 |
| Sodium hypochlorite | 7681-52-9 | 74.44 | 1 M aqueous solution, 15 mL | 15.0 | 1.5 |
| Acetic acid | 64-19-7 | 60.05 | 5 mL | – | – |
| Sodium hydrogen sulfite | 7631-90-5 | 104.06 | aqueous solution, as needed | – | – |

## Procedure

1. Suspend borneol in acetic acid and water.
2. Cool the stirred mixture in a water bath.
3. Add aqueous sodium hypochlorite gradually.
4. Stir for 45 minutes at room temperature.
5. Add aqueous sodium hydrogen sulfite until residual oxidant is destroyed.
6. Set up an apparatus for steam distillation.

## Workup and Purification

Steam-distil the mixture and collect the distillate until little further solid appears. Cool the distillate, filter the camphor and dry it in air.

## Characterization

White crystalline solid; IR (ATR): C=O near 1740 cm⁻¹. Expected ¹H NMR: aliphatic signals at δ 0.8–2.5.

## Notes

1. Acidified hypochlorite can release chlorine; add it slowly in a working fume hood.
2. Check the identity and purity of the product before further use.

## Hazards and Waste Disposal

Borneol (racemic): combustible; may irritate; Sodium hypochlorite: corrosive; releases toxic chlorine with acid; Acetic acid: corrosive; flammable when concentrated; Sodium hydrogen sulfite: harmful if swallowed; releases irritating sulfur dioxide with acid; Camphor (racemic): flammable solid; harmful if swallowed. Work in a fume hood with gloves and eye protection. Collect aqueous and organic waste separately according to local rules.
