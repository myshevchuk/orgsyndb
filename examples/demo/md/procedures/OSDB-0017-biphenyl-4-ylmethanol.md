## Summary

Methyl biphenyl-4-carboxylate is reduced to biphenyl-4-ylmethanol under an inert atmosphere. This is sample data; the quantities are not experimentally verified.

## Reagents

## Procedure

1. Dry the apparatus and establish an inert atmosphere.
2. Add the ester, sodium borohydride and lithium chloride to the flask.
3. Add dry tetrahydrofuran and stir the suspension.
4. Add ethanol slowly while controlling gas evolution.
5. Heat the mixture at reflux for 2 hours under the inert atmosphere.
6. Cool in an ice bath and cautiously add 1 M hydrochloric acid until gas evolution stops.

## Workup and Purification

Add water and extract with ethyl acetate. Dry the organic layer over sodium sulfate, filter and concentrate to obtain the alcohol. Expected yield: 0.64 g (3.50 mmol, 70 %).

## Characterization

White solid; expected IR O–H absorption near 3300 cm⁻¹ and a ¹H NMR CH₂OH signal near δ 4.7.

## Notes

1. Use dry apparatus and solvent before the quench.
2. Ethanol addition and quenching release flammable hydrogen.
3. Keep the apparatus vented and work in a fume hood.

## Hazards and Waste Disposal

Methyl biphenyl-4-carboxylate: may irritate skin and eyes; Sodium borohydride: corrosive; releases flammable hydrogen with water or acid; Lithium chloride: harmful if swallowed; irritates eyes and skin; Tetrahydrofuran: highly flammable; may form explosive peroxides; Ethanol: highly flammable; eye irritant; Ethyl acetate: highly flammable; eye irritant; Hydrogen chloride: acidic solution irritates or burns skin and eyes; Sodium sulfate: dust may irritate eyes and airways; Biphenyl-4-ylmethanol: may irritate skin and eyes. Work in a fume hood; wear gloves and eye protection. Collect organic extracts as non-halogenated waste and the aqueous layer separately.
