## Summary

Anthracene and maleic anhydride form a Diels–Alder cycloadduct. This is sample data; the quantities are not experimentally verified.

## Reagents

| Reagent | CAS | MW (g/mol) | Amount | mmol | Equiv |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anthracene | 120-12-7 | 178.23 | 0.891 g | 5 | 1.0 |
| Maleic anhydride | 108-31-6 | 98.06 | 0.539 g | 5.5 | 1.1 |
| Xylene (isomer mixture) | 1330-20-7 | 106.17 | 20 mL | – | – |

## Procedure

1. Place anthracene and maleic anhydride in a flask.
2. Add most of the xylene and a stirring bar.
3. Fit a reflux condenser open to the atmosphere.
4. Heat at reflux with stirring for 60 minutes.
5. Cool the mixture slowly to room temperature.
6. Cool further in an ice bath to complete crystallisation.

## Workup and Purification

Collect the crystals by suction filtration, wash with the remaining cold xylene and dry. Expected yield: 0.97 g (3.50 mmol, 70 %).

## Characterization

White crystals; expected anhydride C=O absorptions near 1850 and 1770 cm⁻¹.

## Notes

1. Reserve a little xylene for washing.
2. Keep the condenser water flowing during heating.
3. Avoid moisture during storage of the anhydride product.

## Hazards and Waste Disposal

Anthracene: hazardous to aquatic life; dust may irritate; Maleic anhydride: corrosive; may cause respiratory and skin sensitisation; Xylene (isomer mixture): flammable and harmful by inhalation; irritates skin; 9,10-Dihydro-9,10-ethanoanthracene-11,12-dicarboxylic anhydride: may irritate skin and eyes. Work in a fume hood; wear gloves and eye protection. Collect xylene-containing filtrates as organic waste.
