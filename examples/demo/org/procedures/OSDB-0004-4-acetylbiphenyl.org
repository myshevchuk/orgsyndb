* Summary
4-Acetylbiphenyl (1-(biphenyl-4-yl)ethan-1-one) by a ligand-free Suzuki
coupling of 4′-bromoacetophenone with phenylboronic acid in water, with
palladium(II) acetate as the catalyst, potassium carbonate as the base
and tetra-n-butylammonium bromide as a phase-transfer agent. The
product is the starting material of OSDB-0005. This is sample data:
the quantities follow the textbook method but are not experimentally verified.

* Procedure
1. Weigh the potassium carbonate (2.76 g, 20.0 mmol) and the
   tetra-n-butylammonium bromide (1.61 g, 5.00 mmol) into a 100 mL
   round-bottomed flask containing an oval magnetic stirring bar
   (Note 1).
2. Add deionised water (20 mL) and stir until both salts have dissolved.
3. Add the powdered 4′-bromoacetophenone (1.99 g, 10.0 mmol) and the
   phenylboronic acid (1.46 g, 12.0 mmol). Both stay largely undissolved
   at this stage.
4. Bubble nitrogen through the stirred suspension for 10 minutes from a
   long needle reaching to the bottom of the flask, to remove dissolved
   oxygen (Note 2).
5. Add the palladium(II) acetate (11.2 mg, 0.050 mmol) in one portion,
   and rinse the vial into the flask with water (1 mL).
6. Fit a reflux condenser and close it with a nitrogen-filled balloon on
   a needle and septum.
7. Lower the flask into a heating block or water bath preheated to
   70 °C, and stir as fast as the stirring bar allows without splashing
   (Note 3).
8. Within a few minutes the organic solids melt to an oily phase
   dispersed in the water, and within 15 minutes the mixture turns grey
   to black as finely divided palladium separates (Note 4).
9. After 30 minutes, take a sample of the oily phase with a capillary,
   dissolve it in a drop of ethyl acetate and check it by thin-layer
   chromatography on silica (heptane–ethyl acetate 4:1) beside a
   reference spot of 4′-bromoacetophenone (Note 5).
10. Repeat the check every 20 minutes. The reaction is complete when the
    spot of the bromide can no longer be seen, usually within 1 hour.
11. Lift the flask out of the bath and let it cool to room temperature
    with stirring. The product solidifies to a grey, granular cake.
12. Add ethyl acetate (30 mL) and water (20 mL), and stir until the
    solid has dissolved in the organic layer.
13. Filter the two-phase mixture through a pad of Celite (about 2 cm on
    a sintered-glass funnel) under gentle suction, to remove the
    palladium residues. Rinse the flask and the pad with ethyl acetate
    in two 10 mL portions (Note 6).
14. Transfer the filtrate to a separatory funnel and separate the
    layers.
15. Extract the aqueous layer with ethyl acetate (15 mL), and combine
    the organic layers.
16. Wash the combined organic layers with water (20 mL), then with
    saturated sodium chloride solution (20 mL) (Note 7).
17. Dry the organic solution over anhydrous sodium sulfate for
    10 minutes, filter, and rinse the drying agent with ethyl acetate
    (5 mL).
18. Remove the solvent on a rotary evaporator with the bath at 40 °C,
    leaving the crude product as an off-white solid (Note 8).

* Workup and Purification
Dissolve the crude solid in the minimum volume of boiling ethanol, about
10 to 15 mL, added in small portions. If the solution is grey, add a
spatula tip of activated charcoal, boil for one minute and filter it hot
through a fluted filter paper into a warm conical flask. Let the
solution cool slowly to room temperature and then in an ice bath for
15 minutes. Collect the crystals by suction filtration, wash them with
ice-cold ethanol (two 3 mL portions) and dry them under vacuum to
constant mass. Expected yield 1.67 g (8.50 mmol, 85 %). A second crop
can be obtained by concentrating the mother liquor to half its volume,
but it is usually less pure.

* Characterization
Colourless plates. Determine the melting point; a range of 2 °C or less
indicates a pure sample. Thin-layer chromatography (silica, heptane–ethyl
acetate 4:1, UV at 254 nm) shows one spot, distinct from the reference
spot of 4′-bromoacetophenone.

* Notes
1. Tetra-n-butylammonium bromide carries the boronate into the organic
   melt and stabilises the palladium particles. Without it the
   conversion is much slower.
2. Oxygen deactivates the catalyst and promotes the homocoupling of
   phenylboronic acid to biphenyl, which then contaminates the product.
3. The coupling takes place at the surface of the melt, so fast stirring
   matters more than the exact temperature.
4. The dark colour is normal and does not mean that the reaction has
   failed.
5. Visualise the plate under UV light at 254 nm. Spot the reaction
   sample and the reference side by side, and a co-spot of both between
   them.
6. Keep the Celite pad wet with solvent; dry palladium residues on a
   filter aid can ignite solvent vapour. Collect the used pad as
   heavy-metal waste.
7. The 0.2 equivalent excess of phenylboronic acid compensates for its
   slow protodeboronation; the remaining boron compounds are removed in
   the aqueous washes.
8. The product is used as the starting material of OSDB-0005 after
   recrystallisation.

* Hazards and Waste Disposal
4′-Bromoacetophenone, phenylboronic acid and tetra-n-butylammonium
bromide are harmful if swallowed and irritate skin and eyes; weigh them
in a fume hood or a ventilated balance enclosure. Potassium carbonate
irritates skin and eyes. Palladium(II) acetate causes serious eye damage
and may cause an allergic skin reaction; wear gloves and eye protection
and avoid raising dust. Ethyl acetate is highly flammable and its vapour
may cause drowsiness; use it in a fume hood away from ignition sources.
Collect the Celite pad and any palladium residues as heavy-metal solid
waste, because palladium compounds are very toxic to aquatic life.
Neutralise the aqueous layers and dispose of them as aqueous waste
containing heavy metals. Collect the ethyl acetate and ethanol mother
liquors as non-halogenated organic waste.
